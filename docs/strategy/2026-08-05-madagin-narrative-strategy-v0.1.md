## 11. Alternatives and tradeoffs

### Direction A — The Living System (recommended)

**Premise:** One continuous ground-to-canopy composition. Each botanical phase exposes a real layer of Madagin's delivery system. The full tree remains visibly connected to its roots at the moment proof and inquiry appear.

- **Hierarchy/action:** Clear commercial promise and `Start a project` from frame one; story supports rather than delays it.
- **Tone:** Tactile, patient, architectural, cinematic; dark mineral ground opening into soft daylight and controlled blossom color.
- **Benefits:** Best mapping to actual capabilities; naturally explains care; memorable without borrowing a reference's identity.
- **Costs/risks:** Literal growth can become generic or sentimental; botanical continuity is difficult; proof must interrupt the metaphor at the right moment.
- **Responsive consequence:** Desktop can sustain a cross-section; mobile uses discrete poster-led chapters.
- **Reversibility:** High while implemented as layered SVG/stills/segmented clips.
- **Decision-changing evidence:** Reject if owner intent for the blossom is primarily poetic/abstract, or if story walkthroughs cannot explain Madagin within the opening section.

### Direction B — The Field Guide

**Premise:** A contemporary botanical field guide records how a digital presence is cultivated. The tree appears through specimens, annotations, grids, and measured chapter plates rather than one continuous film.

- **Hierarchy/action:** Editorial spreads make capabilities and proof primary; inquiry behaves like an ever-present index tab.
- **Tone:** Scholarly, precise, tactile paper, restrained photography, diagrammatic motion.
- **Benefits:** Easiest to make accessible and performant; accommodates small but deep case studies; less continuity risk.
- **Costs/risks:** Less cinematic; can feel like a familiar editorial portfolio; metaphor may become illustrative rather than experiential.
- **Responsive consequence:** Strong natural reflow into a linear mobile reading path.
- **Reversibility:** Very high.
- **Decision-changing evidence:** Prefer if production budget is limited, identity favors editorial restraint, or literal plant growth feels too generic.

### Direction C — The Threshold Garden

**Premise:** Visitors move through abstract thresholds of soil, shadow, root-like lines, branching light, and drifting petals. A full tree is revealed only near the end.

- **Hierarchy/action:** Short declarative statements and work fragments puncture an atmospheric field; the CTA behaves like a fixed doorway.
- **Tone:** Mysterious, spatial, art-forward, less literal.
- **Benefits:** Strongest differentiation and least sentimental; easier to avoid uncanny botanical transformations.
- **Costs/risks:** Highest comprehension risk; proof and services can feel appended; likely needs more art direction and compositing.
- **Responsive consequence:** Requires a separately composed mobile experience, not crops.
- **Reversibility:** Medium.
- **Decision-changing evidence:** Prefer only if Ocean wants mystery over process clarity and accepts a more selective audience.

**Recommendation:** Direction A, with Direction B as the lower-risk fallback. Prototype A first because its central risk—commercial clarity inside a literal growth story—can be tested cheaply before media production.
